    What's measured and what's modelled

    • Sound is the audio stored in each built DLC for the clip's action, decoded with the same A1800 codec, so it's Furby quality.
    • Timing and busy waits come from running the real show engine against emulate.py's virtual Furbies. A sequence lasts as long as its audio, its delay words or its body movement. Movements have only been timed as ranges: Typical takes the short end and Worst case the long end. Delay-word units are an assumption.
    • Antenna colours are the exact commands the engine sends, at the times it sends them.
    • Eyes are a model: eyecheck.compose()'s reading of how the firmware draws the DLC's eye animations, at a guessed frame rate. Custom eyes are still being debugged on real hardware (left and right eyes that share cels come out garbled), so the real Furby may differ. Between lines the page draws a stand-in for the Furby's built-in eyes.
    • Movement is a wiggle for as long as the sequence keeps the Furby busy, not a picture of the real motor routine.
    • The timer waits a random s between scenes on the night. Play all and Random show wait only a few seconds.
